## 5. Visual direction
- Overall feeling: artistic, sophisticated, editorial, immersive and thoughtfully curated.
- Use the supplied reference images as creative direction; do not copy another brand's identity.
- Prioritise typography, composition, whitespace, image treatment and clear visual hierarchy.
- Avoid generic travel templates and unnecessary visual effects.
- Do not generate AI images for website backgrounds.
- Use the actual logo, photography and assets supplied by the owner.
- Do not invent brand colours, fonts or visual assets if approved references are available.

## 6. Website goals
- Explain the brand and its art-led approach to travel.
- Help visitors discover and understand the available journeys.
- Present Ladakh's destinations and cultural context.
- Make enquiries straightforward.
- Build trust through clear information and a polished experience.

## 7. Planned website pages
- Home
- About
- Journeys / Tours
- Individual journey detail pages
- Interactive Ladakh map
- Art Experience
- Journal / Stories
- Contact / Enquiries

Treat this as a proposed structure. Confirm final page requirements with the owner.

## 8. Journey information
Current journey concepts include itineraries of approximately 10, 13, 14 and 15 days.
Regions and destinations mentioned include Leh, Sham Valley, Nubra Valley, Pangong, Alchi, Nyoma and Changthang.

Use only the itinerary details and business information supplied by the owner.
Do not invent prices, dates, inclusions, accommodation, availability or day-by-day activities.
Keep journey content editable through the admin system.

## 9. Images and media rules
- Use owner-supplied assets wherever available.
- Do not generate AI backgrounds.
- Do not substitute supplied photography with unrelated stock images.
- Use clearly labelled placeholders only when necessary.
- Do not display empty tour galleries on the public website.
- Keep future gallery functionality available so photographs can be added later.
- Optimise image delivery while preserving quality.

## 10. User experience and responsive design
Design intentionally for desktop, tablet and mobile.
Do not merely shrink the desktop layout.
Ensure navigation, typography, forms, maps, galleries and itineraries work at touch-screen sizes.
Avoid horizontal overflow, overlapping elements and inaccessible controls.
Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 11. Motion and interaction
Use subtle, purposeful transitions and animations where they improve the experience.
Prioritise performance and accessibility.
Every visible button, link, form and interactive control must work.

## 12. Admin dashboard and content management
The owner needs a private, secure admin dashboard to manage:
- Website text and page content
- Images and media
- Journeys and day-by-day itineraries
- Journal articles
- Contact details and social links
- Enquiry submissions
- Publishing and unpublishing content

Changes made in the dashboard must persist and update the public website.
Do not build a decorative or non-functional admin interface.
Do not hardcode passwords in frontend code. Use secure authentication and server-side authorisation.

## 13. Technical expectations
Choose a maintainable stack appropriate to the project.
Use reusable components, a persistent database, secure image uploads, validation, accessible markup and SEO-friendly page structure.
Document setup, environment variables, database configuration, admin setup and deployment.
